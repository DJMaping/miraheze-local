Located in a Conservation International biodiversity hotspot as well as the unique [[Virauza Floristic Region]], the city of [[Vurahi]] has one of the highest levels of biodiversity of any equivalent area in the world.

These protected areas are a World Heritage Site, and an estimated 2,200 species of plants are confined to the [[Seat of Vurahi]] – more than exist in the whole of [[Emara]] which has 1200 plant species and 67 endemic plant species. Many of these species, including a great many types of proteas, are endemic to the mountain and can be found nowhere else.

It is home to a total of 19 different vegetation types, of which several are endemic to the city and occur nowhere else in the world. It is also the only habitat of hundreds of endemic species, and hundreds of others which are severely restricted or threatened. This enormous species diversity is mainly because the city is uniquely located at the convergence point of several different soil types and micro-climates.

The [[Seat of Vurahi]] has an unusually rich biodiversity. Its vegetation consists predominantly of several different types of the unique and rich Vurahi Fynbos. The main vegetation type is endangered Vurahi Sandstone Fynbos, but critically endangered Vurahi Granite Fynbos, Vurahi Shale Renosterveld and Afromontane forest occur in smaller portions on the mountain.

Rapid population growth and urban sprawl has covered much of these ecosystems with development. Consequently, [[Vurahi]] has over 300 threatened plant species and 13 which are now extinct. [[The Virauza plain]] has the highest concentration of threatened species of any continental area of equivalent size in the world.

Tiny remnant populations of critically endangered or near extinct plants sometimes survive on road sides, pavements and sports fields. The remaining ecosystems are partially protected through a system of over 30 nature reserves – including the massive [[seat of vurahi National Park]].

A worldwide survey suggested that the extinction rate of endemic plants from [[Vurahi]] is one of the highest in the world, at roughly three per year since 1700 – partly a consequence of the very small and localized habitats and high endemicity
