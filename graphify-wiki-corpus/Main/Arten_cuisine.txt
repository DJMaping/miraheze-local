Vurahis's cuisine has evolved through centuries and periods of social, cultural, and political changes. Vurahi became a major gastronomical centre during the ancient age. Ancient Vury cuisine was highly influenced by nearby tribes, and after, the empire's enormous expansion exposed Vurys to many new, provincial culinary habits and cooking techniques.

Food originating from or synonymous with [[Vurahi]] includes the savoury sweet spiced meat dish [[Vobotie]] that dates from the 13th century. The Gatsby, a sandwich filled with slap chips and other toppings, was first served in 1717 in the suburb of [[Nor Handa]] and is also synonymous with the city. The Cho'sister is a traditional Arten pastry described as a cinnamon infused dumpling with a cake-like texture, finished off with a sprinkling of desiccated coconut.

[[The Porvuka]], Vurahi's trade and slaughterhouse area, was often known as the "belly" or "slaughterhouse" of Vurahi, and was inhabited by butchers, or ''Porvuks''. The old-fashioned [[Perkant]] (oxtail cooked in the way of butchers) is still one of the city's most popular meals and is part of most of Vurahis's restaurants' menus. Lamb is also a very popular part of Vury cuisine, and is often roasted with spices and herbs.

Malva pudding (sometimes known as Arten Malva pudding) is a sticky sweet dessert often served with hot custard is also associated with the city and dates back to the 12th century. A related dessert dish, [[Vury Brandy Pudding]], is also associated with the city and surrounding region. Vurahi is also the home of the [[Arten wine]] industry with the first wine produced in the country being bottled in the city; a number of notable wineries still exist in the city including [[Nor Havan]] and [[Chur Havan]].
