The '''Koaissa desert''' is the world's second largest [[wikipedia:Desert_climate|hot desert]], covering 5,719,652 square kilometres (3,554,026 sq mi) of [[Massir]]. It ranks as the second-largest desert globally, exceeded in area only by the [[Alomara|Alomaran Desert]]. The name is derived from the [[Alquan Language|Alquan]] Koaissa, the plural form of koais, which translates literally as "desert."

The region encompasses most of [[North Massir|North]] and [[South Massir]], except the fertile areas of the [[The Mare|Mare]], [[Chuanyi]], and the [[Kelias Mountains]]. It is bounded by the [[Kera Sea]] to the north east, the [[Ubrana Sea|Ubrana]] and [[Trayd Cren sea|Trayd Cren]] Seas to the east, [[Hingan Steppe]] and [[Kozhika Mountains]] to the west, and the [[Kashmyar Steppe]] to the south. Distinctive geographic regions include the [[Tautan]], [[Eibosati]], and [[Zhalrn mountains]], as well as the [[Serwis Desert|Serwis]] and [[Liqbai Desert|Liqbai Deserts]].

== Geography ==
The Koaissa covers approximately 5,719,652 million square kilometres (3,554,026 sq mi), representing one third of the Massiran continent. This area expands to ______  million square kilometres when defined by regions receiving less than 250 mm of annual [[wikipedia:Precipitation|precipitation]]. Spanning numerous nations from [[Haiza]] to [[Vesozata]], the Koaissa is a primary physiographic province of [[Massir]].

The landscape is defined by [[wikipedia:Hamada rocky plateaus]] and gravel plains, with [[wikipedia:Erg_(landform)|sand seas]] constituting a smaller portion than commonly perceived. Diverse features include dunes exceeding 180 metres (590 ft), dry valleys, and salt flats, shaped primarily by eolian processes and infrequent rainfall. Notable landmarks include the [[Laoxois Structure]] in [[Migoku]] and volcanic massifs such as the [[Eibosati Mountains|Eibosati]] and [[Tautan Mountains]], the latter containing Koaissa's highest peak.

The hyperarid central Koaissa supports minimal vegetation and may go years without rainfall. In contrast, the western and eastern fringes host sparse grasslands and desert shrubs. Moisture-collecting wadis often support taller shrubs and trees, providing a transition between the core desert and more temperate surrounding regions.

== Climate ==
The Koaissa, identified as the world's second-hottest desert, is situated primarily within the southern latitudes. Its hyper-arid climate is governed by the subtropical ridge, a semi-permanent belt of high pressure. In this region, air from the [[wikipedia:Troposphere|upper troposphere]] descends to the surface, a process that compresses, warms, and dries the lower atmosphere. This atmospheric subsidence effectively suppresses cloud formation and prevents the convection necessary for rainfall.

The persistent lack of cloud cover in the northern half results in high levels of [[wikipedia:Solar_irradiance|solar]] and [[wikipedia:Thermal_radiation|thermal radiation]] reaching the surface. The stability of the Koaissan atmosphere inhibits convective overturning, creating a weather pattern characterised by extreme dryness and stability. The dry, diverging air masses linked to these subtropical [[wikipedia:High-pressure_area|high-pressure systems]] are highly unfavourable for the development of convectional showers. This mechanism is the primary driver of the Köppen climate classification BWh (hot desert climate) that defines the region. Contrasting the hyper-arid heat of the north, the southern expanse transitions into a [[wikipedia:Desert_climate#Cold_desert_climates|cold desert]].

=== Flora and fauna ===
The flora of the Koaissa is highly diversified based on the biogeographical characteristics of this vast desert. Floristically, the Koaissa has [[Four zones of Koaissa|four zones]] based on the amount of rainfall received – the [[Northern Koaissan zone|Northern]], [[North Central Koaissan zone|North Central,]] [[South Koaissan zone|South]] and [[West Koaissan zone|West]] Zones. There are two transitional zones – the [[North-south Koaissan transition zone|north-south transition]] and the [[South-west Koaissan transition zone|south-west transition zon]]<nowiki/>e. The Koaissan flora comprises around [[Species in Koaissa|4800 species]] of vascular plants. Approximately a quarter of these are endemic. About half of these species are common to the flora of the [[Hiemuekan desert]].

The central Koaissa is estimated to include [[Species of plants in Koaissa|seven hundred species of plants]], which is extremely low considering the huge extent of the area. Plants such as trees, palms, succulents, spiny shrubs, and grasses have adapted to the arid conditions, by growing lower to avoid water loss by strong winds, by storing water in their thick stems to use it in dry periods, by having long roots that travel horizontally to reach the maximum area of water and to find any surface moisture, and by having small thick leaves or needles to prevent water loss by [[wikipedia:Evapotranspiration|evapotranspiration]]. Plant leaves may dry out totally and then recover.
