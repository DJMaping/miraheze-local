The '''Siita Desert''' is a vast [[wikipedia:Desert|desert]] wilderness spanning across a large portion of [[North Acrola]] in Acrola, covering 2,094,992 square kilometers (1,301,767 sq mi). It is the [[List of deserts by area|fifth-largest desert]] in the world and the largest in [[Acrola]], stretching from [[Jau]] to Areoix Lie.

At its core is [[Egrata Lwio]] (The Empty Expance), one of the largest continuous bodies of sand globally. Sometimes, it is considered an extension of the [[Taiylsh Desert]]. called the [[Greated Taiylsh Desert]]

==Geography==
The Siita Desert is a vast expanse characterised by diverse topographical features, including [[wikipedia:Dune|massive sand dunes]] (reaching over 250 meters in height), extensive [[wikipedia:Gravel|gravel plains]], and scattered [[wikipedia:Outcrop|rocky outcrops]]. The desert's core is dominated by [[Egrata Lwio]] (The Empty Expance), which represents one of the largest continuous bodies of sand in the world. The terrain slopes generally upwards towards the [[Valmiran Mountains]]. [[wikipedia:Wadi|Dry riverbeds]] crisscross the landscape, carrying water only during rare, intense flash floods, and are critical features for local [[wikipedia:Hydrology|hydrology]] and vegetation. The desert's geography is defined by a [[wikipedia:Desert_climate|hyper-arid climate]], leading to negligible surface water and landscapes shaped almost entirely by aeolian processes (wind action).
==Climate==
The climate is predominantly dry, with most areas receiving around 100 mm (3.9 in) of annual rain, though some rare locations receive as little as 50 mm. Temperatures fluctuate dramatically, ranging from very high heat to seasonal nighttime freezes.

The desert supports adapted species such as 0000, 0000, sand 0000, and s00000, surviving in an environment featuring red dunes and [[wikipedia:Quicksand|quicksand]]. It belongs to the desert and shrublands biome.
===Flora and fauna===
The ecoregion has limited biodiversity and features few endemic plants. Historically present species like the striped [[Slin]], [[Buthro]] and [[Siita sand Yuant]] have gone extinct locally due to factors including hunting, habitat destruction, overgrazing, off-road driving, and human encroachment. Conservation efforts, such as the successful reintroduction of the [[Siita sand Yuant]], are ongoing in protected reserves.
