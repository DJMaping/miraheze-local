STORY OF THE TOURNAMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The 1724 tournament was the first FLLA World Cup held outside the established
football heartlands - Areoix Lie, a baseball-mad host with a modest FLLA
ranking, won the bid in 1721 on a wave of expansion enthusiasm. The 24-team
field, two-thirds larger than the 16-team line-up of 1720, was the FLLA
Council's response to growing pressure from smaller associations for greater
representation.

The format itself proved to be the story. Six first-round groups of four were
uncontroversial - it was the second stage that drew the ire of players and
press alike. Twelve advancers were drawn into four groups of three, with only
the group winner advancing to the Semi-Finals. With just three matches per
group and a rotating bye, last-round collusion was a constant suspicion;
three of the four second-round groups produced a final-day match in which
one side already knew exactly what result it needed to advance.

Verusa navigated the format with calm precision. Top of their first-round
group, top of their second-round group of three, they did not face a single
knockout match until the Semi-Final - their attacking play, modelled on the
"total football" philosophy that swept through the upper FLLA tier in the
early 1720s, swept aside every opponent. The 3-1 final scoreline over
Easuhura was decisive without flattering them.

For the hosts, Areoix Lie (FLLA #56), the campaign was a triumph of spirit.
A nation whose sporting culture is built around baseball - the FLLA's debuff
system rates their football programme accordingly - they were never expected to
trouble the established powers. Yet they advanced from their first-round group
on home soil and pushed into the awkward second stage, where the format's
randomness cut both ways.
By the time they bowed out at the 3rd Place Playoff, they had become
the tournament's defining Cinderella - though many in the FLLA senior
associations took the deep run as further evidence that the awkward
bracket was producing the wrong sort of fairy-tale: not a deserving
minnow on form, but a passable side enriched by the luck of the draw.

The format - and only the format - was the headline story by the closing
weekend. By March 1726 the FLLA Council had voted to revert to a 16-team
field for 1728. The 1724 edition remains the only FLLA World Cup contested
under the "groups of three" second stage.
