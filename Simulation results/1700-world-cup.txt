STORY OF THE TOURNAMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The 8th edition arrived after the longest interruption in the competition's
history. Three consecutive scheduled tournaments — 1688, 1692 and 1696 — had
been called off as the Red War spread across half the continents in turn. For
sixteen years the trophy sat in storage. When the FLLA Council finally met in
Tirna in February 1699, they did two things at once: they confirmed Raledria
as host, and they formally renamed the competition. From this edition forward,
it would be known as the FLLA World Cup, recognising that teams from every
then-existing confederation now competed for it.

It was the first time the trophy had been contested in Atirha. The crowds
came in numbers nobody had planned for; the Tirna National Stadium took the
largest single-match attendance ever recorded at an international fixture —
an estimated 38,000 — for the Final.

The football itself was cautious. Sixteen years of war had emptied training
pitches and disrupted coaching cycles; teams arrived under-prepared, drilled
more in defensive shape than attacking patterns. The tournament average of
2.91 goals per match reflected the era — measured, careful football, marked
by long stretches of midfield play and decisive moments in narrow margins.

Raledria, the hosts, found themselves carrying a nation's expectations into
each fixture. They were not the strongest side in the field by the adjusted
ratings — Ayuman opposition arrived with their wartime structures more
intact — but at home, in front of capacity crowds, they ground out result
after result. By the Final they had become unbeaten through six rounds.

The Final at the Tirna National Stadium was the tournament's defining match.
Raledria opened the scoring before half-time, United Delet pulled one back
in the second half, and the home side then put two more past the South-
American visitors to win 3-1. With it, Raledria lifted the trophy on
home soil in the first edition under the FLLA World Cup name — a fitting
inverse of the Maracanazo: where Earth's 1950 hosts suffered at home,
Raledria triumphed at home.

The shock of the group stage came from Pelines (FLLA #35), who stunned
Merela Sta 1-0 in Group B — a result that drew comparisons to
the United States' famous 1-0 victory over England at Earth's 1950 tournament.
Pelines bowed out at the group stage but went home as heroes, the upset of
the tournament.

The Golden Boot was claimed by a Raledrian striker with 9 goals — an
individual tally in the same range as Ademir's 9 at Earth's 1950 tournament.
