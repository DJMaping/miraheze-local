STORY OF THE TOURNAMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The 10th FLLA World Cup was a landmark in every sense. For the first time,
the tournament was awarded outside the established hubs of Ayuma and Atirha —
hosted by United Delet, in New Ayre. For the first time, photojournalists from
all seven confederations were granted match-day access, and selected fixtures
were relayed live by short-range radio to neighbouring nations. By the end of
the closing ceremony, the FLLA World Cup had become a continental event in a
way it never had been before.

The football matched the occasion. Defending champions Raledria arrived in
United Delet as overwhelming favourites — they had lifted the trophy four
years earlier in 1704 and were unbeaten in competitive fixtures going into
the tournament. They reached the Final on schedule, navigating each round
without ever looking pushed.

What they had not accounted for was the host. United Delet (FLLA #10)
played the tournament of their lives. A breakout young attacker — barely out
of his teens, marked from his second group fixture onwards — gave the side a
focal point in the box that no opponent could nullify. The Pelé comparison
would be made over and over in the years that followed.

The Final at the National Stadium in United Delet drew the largest live
audience FLLA football had ever seen. United Delet struck early, conceded
once on the counter, then added two more in the second half to seal a
3-1 victory. Raledria, the defending champions and pre-tournament
favourites, were beaten — and a host nation lifted the FLLA World Cup
trophy for the first time outside Ayuma or Atirha.

Emara took the bronze, beating New Misos 1-0 in the 3rd-place playoff.
But the individual story of the tournament belonged to the Emaran striker
who finished with 13 goals — a tally not approached by any other player.
He took the Golden Boot home with the kind of personal campaign — open running,
ruthless finishing, hat-tricks in successive rounds — that drew immediate
Just-Fontaine comparisons from the photojournalists covering the tournament.
