STORY OF THE TOURNAMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The 11th FLLA World Cup, hosted by Praesyu, opened to perfect late-summer
weather and a public mood to match. Federal capital sides arrived expecting
attacking football and got it; from the opening fixtures through to the
closing whistle, this would be a tournament defined by goals.

The 3.31 goals-per-match average — the highest the FLLA World Cup had ever
produced — would stand as a record not equalled until the 1748 edition.
Defensive structures buckled all over the bracket; the spectacle, viewers
agreed, was the football of the future.

At the heart of it was Raledria, returning four years on from their 1704
triumph, hungry to reclaim the trophy they had surrendered to United Delet in
the 1708 final. They were the most complete attacking unit in the field and
played like a side that knew it, slicing through deep blocks with sharp
vertical passing and pulling defenders out of position with movement off the
ball. They reached the Final unbeaten through six rounds of football.

The Final at the National Stadium in Praesyu — the country's largest venue,
set in the federal capital — was the showpiece the tournament deserved.
Raledria struck early, were pegged back, then put three more past Easuhura in
a punishing second-half display to win 4-2. It was their second FLLA
World Cup title, four years after their first.

Host Praesyu had a tournament to remember. After winning Group C, they
dispatched their Quarter-Final opponent and pushed Raledria to the limit in
the semi-final, eventually losing 3-2 to the eventual champions. In
the third-place playoff they beat United Delet 3-1 to secure the
bronze medal — a result that fits the same mould as Chile's 3rd-place finish
at Earth's 1962 tournament.

The tournament also produced its share of physical confrontation. The
Group C meeting between Praesyu and Trian, finishing 3-4, was the
most ill-tempered fixture of the competition — referees issued repeated
warnings, the match was halted twice, and the post-match analysis drew
comparisons to the so-called Battle of Santiago at Earth's 1962 tournament.

Individual honours went to the Raledrian attacking talisman, whose 9
goals took home the Golden Boot. His combination of pace, dribbling and
finishing — Garrincha at Earth 1962 was the obvious comparison — was the
individual story of the competition.
