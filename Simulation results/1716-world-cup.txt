STORY OF THE TOURNAMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The 12th FLLA World Cup will be remembered first for what nearly stopped it.
Hosting rights had been awarded to Estijan in 1712, but in November 1714 the
FLLA Council withdrew them after a coordinated programme of performance-
enhancing pharmaceutical use was uncovered in the Estijan domestic league.
Estijan was barred from the competition entirely, and Easuhura took the
tournament at four months' notice — an administrative scramble that no host
before or since has had to attempt.

The football itself was cagey, tactical, and low-scoring. Coaches arrived
drilled in deep blocks; defenders won the headlines as often as forwards.
The 2.47 goals-per-match average was the lowest in any FLLA World Cup
to date, with several knockout fixtures decided by a single late goal.

Above the noise, Raledria continued their dynasty. Already champions in 1704
and 1712, they navigated the group stage and three knockout rounds with the
composure of a side who had been here before. The final against Emara at the
National Arena in Easuhura was tied at 1-1 after ninety minutes — Emara's
equaliser late in the second half forcing extra time — before Raledria struck
the winner in the second period of extra time to seal a 2-1 after extra
time. With it, they became the first side to lift the FLLA World Cup three times.

The shock of the group stage came from Terressin (FLLA #27), who stunned
Verusa 1-0 in Group A — the kind of result that drew comparisons
to North Korea's famous 1-0 over Italy at Earth's 1966 tournament. They
ultimately fell short of the knockout rounds but exited as heroes back home.

The Golden Boot went to Lycroa (FLLA #2, Ayuma), whose striker bagged
10 goals across the campaign before bowing out at the semi-final stage —
an Eusebio-style individual tournament that lit up an otherwise tightly
contested competition.

Host Easuhura topped Group C and reached the Quarter-Finals before bowing out to Etretes 1-3.
